[Title]
On the Conservation Laws and Pathway Realizability of Biochemical Reaction Networks

[Abstract]
Background: Conserved metabolite pools and substrate availability constrain biochemical pathways before kinetic parameters are known. These properties are usually evaluated on incompatible mathematical abstractions: stoichiometric nullspaces omit shared catalytic participants, while graph reachability ignores token multiplicities. Because participants that appear identically on both reaction sides are eliminated under net stoichiometry despite being required for firing, standard reductions can destroy catalytic gating and alter deficiency.

Results: We present the chemical reaction network (CRN) submodule of SynKit, an open-source Python toolkit with an optimized C++ core. Networks imported from KEGG, structural SBML models, or generative rules share a bipartite representation preserving distinct reactant and product incidences. The toolkit computes exact conservation laws, minimal semiflows and siphons, deficiency, and discrete firing certificates for prescribed reaction multisets. Twelve reference networks match analytical values and agree with CRNT4SBML across eight structural invariants. Ten reaction-bearing BioModels entries, up to 504 species and 1,654 directed reactions, pass structural round trips. In one model, cancelling shared catalysts leaves net stoichiometry unchanged but lowers deficiency from 13 to 11. Matched formose expansions reach identical sets of 10,572 species and 11,239 reactions across six depths in SynKit, MØD, and MolFoundry. For a prescribed glycolytic flux with ADP, NAD+ and phosphate supplied, one initial ATP gives a non-negative predicted final inventory yet blocks execution. Two initial ATP permit a 15-step certificate. In the 37-species formose network, conservation weights reproduce carbon counts. Two prescribed target routes require two or three glycolaldehyde molecules and consume no formaldehyde, explaining their different outcomes across inventories with equal carbon budgets.

Conclusions: Stoichiometric conservation is necessary but insufficient to guarantee pathway execution. By unifying conservation analysis, deficiency invariants, and Petri-net reachability on a single incidence representation with exact rational arithmetic, SynKit makes participant retention, cofactor boundaries, and precursor constraints explicit and testable. Source code, recorded benchmarks, and reproducible analysis workflows accompany the manuscript.

[Cover letter — draft for BMC Bioinformatics]
Dear Editor,

We submit our manuscript entitled "On the Conservation Laws and Pathway Realizability of Biochemical Reaction Networks" for consideration as a Software article in BMC Bioinformatics.

The manuscript presents the chemical reaction network submodule of SynKit, an open-source Python toolkit with an optimized C++ core. It combines exact conservation analysis, minimal semiflow and siphon enumeration, chemical reaction network deficiency, and discrete pathway verification on a shared representation that retains separate reactant and product incidences. This allows researchers to examine conservation, catalytic requirements, and finite substrate inventories without changing the underlying reaction model between analyses.

We validate structural quantities on twelve reference networks and compare eight invariants with CRNT4SBML. Ten reaction-bearing BioModels entries pass structural SBML round trips. Matched formose expansions agree with MØD and MolFoundry on the generated species and reaction sets. Glycolysis and formose case studies demonstrate why a balanced net conversion or conserved carbon budget alone cannot establish that a prescribed reaction sequence can execute from a specified initial inventory.

The work addresses computational analysis and modelling of biochemical networks, with applications in systems biology and pathway analysis. It provides reproducible workflows and explicit certificates that connect structural network properties to discrete pathway feasibility. The software is distributed under the MIT License at https://github.com/TieuLongPhan/SynKit. Experiments are available at https://github.com/TieuLongPhan/SynKit/tree/v1.7.0/Experiment/CRN. This study uses version 1.7.0 (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24135298).
